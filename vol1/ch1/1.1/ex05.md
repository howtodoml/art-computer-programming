# TAOCP Vol. 1, Section 1.1, Exercise 5 [12]

**Problem (paraphrased).** The flowchart "Procedure for Reading This Set of Books", printed after the preface, isn't a real algorithm. Show that it fails at least three of the five properties of an algorithm. Also point out how its format differs from Algorithm E.

The five properties are finiteness, definiteness, input, output and effectiveness. Box numbers below refer to the boxes in the flowchart.

## It isn't finite

An algorithm has to stop after a finite number of steps. This one has no stopping box at all. Every box has an arrow leading out of it.

The obvious candidate is box 18, "Relax". But its arrow goes straight back to box 3, which sets N ← 1 and starts you on Chapter 1 again. So after you finish the last chapter, you're sent back to read the whole set from the start, forever.

There are smaller loops too. Boxes 12 and 13 ("Work exercises" and "Check answers") point at each other, and nothing forces you out. Someone who keeps getting answers wrong can go round that loop indefinitely.

## It isn't definite

Every step has to say exactly what to do, with no room for interpretation.

- Box 5 asks "Interesting?" and box 14 asks "Tired?". Two readers will answer these differently, and the same reader may answer differently on different days.
- Box 8 asks whether the section is starred. Its "Yes" arrow is labelled "First time", which means you skip starred sections on the first read. But the chart never says what to do with a starred section the second time, and it doesn't say how to tell which time it is.
- Box 16 says "Increase N" without saying by how much.
- Box 7 has an exit labelled "End of chapter", but the chart doesn't say when that test is made or what counts as the end.
- "Skim math", "Relax" and "Sleep" have no precise meaning.

## It isn't effective

Each step has to be basic enough to do exactly, in a finite time, with pencil and paper.

Box 12, "Work exercises", fails this. Some exercises in these books are research problems, and some were unsolved when they were set. Nobody can promise to finish those in a finite amount of time. "Check formulas" (box 10) has the same problem on a smaller scale: checking a hard derivation can take as long as you like.

## Input and output are unclear

These two are weaker points, but they're worth mentioning. The chart doesn't name its input. Presumably it's the books, plus a reader, which isn't a quantity in the usual sense. And it produces no output. The nearest thing is the reader's understanding, which the procedure never states or checks. Since it never stops, it couldn't deliver an output anyway.

## How the format differs from Algorithm E

- It's drawn as a flowchart. Algorithm E is written as numbered lines of text.
- The steps are numbered 1 to 18 with no letter in front. Algorithm E's steps are E1, E2, E3, so you always know which algorithm a step belongs to.
- There's no bracketed summary at the start of each step, like E1's [Find remainder.].
- There's no opening sentence naming the algorithm and saying what it takes in and gives back.
- Control moves along arrows instead of explicit "go back to step E1" or "the algorithm terminates" instructions.
- Tests are drawn as rounded boxes with Yes/No exits instead of being written as "If …, then …".
- It has no comments in parentheses, like E1's note that 0 ≤ r < n.

## Check

`ex05.py` encodes the flowchart's arrows as a graph. It checks that every box has at least one exit, so there's nowhere to stop. It also checks that box 18 leads back to box 3, and that boxes 12 and 13 form a loop.
